About Campus IQ

Built by students, kept by students.

Campus IQ started in 2019 as a WhatsApp group that answered one question very well — “where is the room again?”. Seven years later it runs on the university's own data, under the university's supervision, and still answers like a friend would.

The short version

One group chat became a platform.

Freshers ask the same questions every September — where the print lab is, what the shuttle does, when forms close. So in 2019, a computing student and a librarian started answering them, together, in one place.

The university saw it, funded it, and opened its own systems. Today IQ reads official timetables, registries and calendars directly — the friendliness stayed, the guesswork didn't.

What we believe

Four rules we don't break.

01

Official or it doesn't ship

If an answer comes from a system the university owns, we say so. If it comes from a student's memory, we say that too. No silent mixing.

02

Fast beats clever

You're not in the library when you need it — you're walking to the shuttle. Every screen answers in one glance or less.

03

Your data isn't our content

Grades, fees, location — none of it becomes public material. Questions improve answers for everyone; they never identify you.

04

Built on campus, for campus

The team works in the Student Centre. When something breaks, a student with a fix is usually five chairs away.

The long version

Seven years, in five chapters.

2019

The roof-garden group chat

14 students, one WhatsApp group, a shared doc of room numbers. The library roof garden was the HQ because it had power and shade.

2021

First official data feed

The academic office opened the timetable feed. IQ stopped asking for screenshots and started reading the source. Answer accuracy jumped overnight.

2023

Events, dining and shuttles

Student Affairs, the dining ledger and the transport team all connected. The platform stopped being an answer machine and became a map of the whole term.

2025

IQ learns Kiswahili

The assistant started answering in the language the campus actually speaks. Usage on weekends tripled. The team was mildly delighted.

2026

12,400 students and counting

Every campus service now lives on one platform, and the team — now nine, with two staff mentors — runs it out of the Student Centre, floor one.
